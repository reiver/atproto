How-To Count an ATProto Account's Followers: A Developers' Guide

app.bsky.graph.follow app.bsky.actor.getProfile blue.microcosm.links.getBacklinksCount

by Charles Iliya Krempeaux

This article goes over how-to count the followers of an AT-Protocol (ATProto) account from a computer-programming point-of-view.

It sounds simple. It is not — because, in ATProto, no single place has the answer.

Nobody “has” an account's follower count — not the account's own PDS, and not any one service. Every follower count you see is somebody's estimate, made by gathering follows from across the whole network. Different services get different numbers.

Where Follows Are Stored

A follow is a record, of type app.bsky.graph.follow. And, it is stored in the follower's repository — not in the repository of the account being followed.

For example, here is one of @bsky.app's follows — a record in @bsky.app's own repository (with the followed account's DID left out):

The subject is the DID of the account being followed. The lexicon describes it as a:

Record declaring a social 'follow' relationship of another account. Duplicate follows will be ignored by the AppView.

So:

  • An account's PDS knows exactly who that account follows — those records are in its repository.
  • But, it does not know who follows it. Those records are spread across everyone else's repositories — on every PDS in the network.

Ways to Count Followers

To count an account's followers, you have to find every app.bsky.graph.follow record — in every repository — whose subject is that account. There are 3 ways to go about that.

Way 1: Check Every Repository

This works, in principle:

  1. get a list of every account — for example, with com.atproto.sync.listRepos on a relay,
  2. download each account's repository — with com.atproto.sync.getRepo (see: How-To Backup an ATProto PDS), and
  3. count the follow records whose subject is the account.

But, it is very expensive — it means downloading tens of millions of repositories. And, you can never be sure you found every PDS — a relay only knows the hosts it crawls. By the time you finish, the answer has changed, too.

Way 2: Index the Firehose (What AppViews Do)

Instead of scanning every time someone asks, keep a running count:

  1. fill in the past once (a backfill — Way 1, done one time), then
  2. watch the firehose, and add 1 for every new follow, and subtract 1 for every deleted follow.

Then, answering “how many followers?” is just a lookup. This is what an AppView does — and the count it returns is in followersCount, from app.bsky.actor.getProfile. (The list of followers comes from app.bsky.graph.getFollowers.)

How complete the count is depends on which relays the AppView reads, and how complete its backfill was.

Way 3: Ask a Backlink Index

A backlink index keeps track of every link from every record — organized by what the link points to. Then, “how many follow records point at this DID?” is just a lookup.

Constellation, from the microcosm project, is one. It describes itself as “a self-hosted JSON API to an atproto-wide index of PDS record back-links”, and says:

It works by recursively walking all records coming through the firehose, searching for anything that looks like a link. Links are indexed by the target they point at, the collection the record came from, and the JSON path to the link in that record.

To count follows of an account:

source is the collection (app.bsky.graph.follow), and the field in the record that holds the link (subject).

Note that, at the time of writing, its home page says “indexing new records in real time, backfill coming soon!” — and that it has indexed for 662 days. So, it only knows about follows created (or seen on the firehose) since it started.

Why the Numbers Differ

coverage

Which relays and PDSes each service reads, since when, and whether it filled in the past. (Constellation, for example, has not backfilled.)

policy

What each service chooses to count. For example, the follow lexicon says “Duplicate follows will be ignored by the AppView.” And, a service might leave out follows from accounts that are deleted, deactivated, taken down, or that it considers spam. (I am inferring this from how these services generally work — I have not confirmed the actual rules each of these services uses.)

timing

Each service updates on its own schedule.

None of them is “wrong”. Each is counting what it has seen, by its rules. (See also: Falsehoods Programmers Believe About ATProto.)

Summary

  1. A follow is an app.bsky.graph.follow record, stored in the follower's repository.
  2. So, no PDS knows an account's followers — only who the account follows.
  3. To count followers, you have to find follow records across the whole network — by checking every repository, by indexing the firehose (what AppViews do), or by asking a backlink index (like Constellation).
  4. Different services give different numbers, because of coverage, policy, and timing.
  5. So, a follower count is an estimate — not a fact.
